# Usability Improvements

## Frontend Improvement 1
- Problem: The Products page showed all 102 items with only category chips. A shopper who wanted "something with a bulldog", "a hoodie that's actually in stock in XL", or "gear under $60" had to scroll through everything and open product pages one by one to check sizes. Filters also reset whenever they went to a product and pressed Back.
- Change implemented: A filter bar on the Products page, all working on real catalogue data from `/api/products`:
  - **Search box** matching name, garment type, category, description, colors and search tags (the same fields the chat agent searches), e.g. "bulldog", "Saybrook", "hockey", "gray".
  - **"Available in size"** filter (XS–XXL) that keeps only products with quantity > 0 in that size. `/api/products` now returns each product's `in_stock_sizes`, computed from the `inventory` table.
  - **Max price** filter (under $40 / $60 / $80 / $100) and **Sort** (name, price low→high, price high→low).
  - Live "N of 102 items" count, a **Clear filters** chip, and an empty state with a "Clear all filters" button instead of a blank page.
  - All filters are stored in the **URL** (`/products?category=Hoodies&size=XL&max=60`), so Back, refresh and shared links keep them.
- Why it improves usability: The most common shopping questions (what fits my size, my budget, my interest) can be answered in one or two clicks without opening each product. Filtering by in-stock size means shoppers stop landing on items that are sold out in their size, which is less frustrating for them and more likely to end in a sale for the store.
- Files changed: `frontend/src/pages/Products.tsx`, `frontend/src/types.ts`, `frontend/src/index.css`, `backend/main.py` (`in_stock_sizes` in `/api/products`).
- Tested: "bulldog" → 10 items (DB: 10). Hoodies → 27. Hoodies + XL → 21 (DB: 21). Hoodies + XL + under $60 → only Yale Sports Hoodie Tennis (DB: same). Price high→low starts at $98.00. "pink" → 0 with the empty state. The URL updated to `?category=Hoodies&size=XL&max=60`, and opening that URL directly restored the filters.

## Frontend Improvement 2
- Problem: The assistant could already understand "this" on a product page (Problem 8), but shoppers couldn't *see* that, so they didn't know they could just ask "is this in M?". A brand-new chat was also an empty box: people had to guess what the bot can do, and on a product page there was no obvious link between the item and the chat.
- Change implemented:
  - **"Viewing: <product name>" bar** in the chat panel whenever a product page is open. The name is looked up from the DB by `product_id`, so the shopper sees exactly what "this" refers to.
  - **One-click suggested questions** above the input. On a product page: "Is this in stock in M?", "What colors does this come in?", "Show me similar items". Elsewhere: "What hoodies do you have?", "T-shirts under $40 in size M", "Compare two quarter-zips". Clicking one sends it immediately. They are hidden while a reply is loading, so they can't cause duplicate sends.
  - **"Ask the assistant about this item" button** on every product detail page. It opens the chat (via a small window event), with the product already in context.
- Why it improves usability: It shows what the assistant can do (stock by size, colors, budget filters, comparisons) instead of making people guess, and turns a product question into one click. The visible context bar prevents confusion about which item "this" means, especially after browsing several pages.
- Files changed: `frontend/src/components/ChatWidget.tsx`, `frontend/src/chatEvents.ts` (new), `frontend/src/pages/ProductDetail.tsx`, `frontend/src/index.css`.
- Tested: On `/products/crew-left-chest-hoodie`, "Ask the assistant about this item" opened the closed chat. The bar showed "Viewing: Crew Left Chest Hoodie" with the three product suggestions. Clicking "Is this in stock in M?" sent it and returned "Sorry, size M is out of stock. It's currently available in XS, S, L, XL, and XXL." plus the product card (DB: M = 0). On Home, the general suggestions appear and there is no context bar.

## Agent / Backend Improvement 1
- Problem: When a shopper asked "What's the difference between these two?" or "Which is cheaper?", the agent had to call `get_product_details` and `get_inventory` separately for each item and then do the math itself, which is slow and error-prone. Without structure it was also tempted to fill the gap with unsupported claims ("warmer", "better quality").
- Change implemented: A new database-backed tool, **`compare_products(product_ids)`** (2–4 items), returning a typed `ProductComparison`. Per product: price, garment type, category, colors, total stock, in-stock sizes and out-of-stock sizes. Computed across products: `cheapest`, `price_difference`, `colors_in_common`, `sizes_in_stock_for_all`, and `not_found` for bad ids. Its note tells the model to compare only these fields. The prompt says when to use it, to base any recommendation on the shopper's stated needs (budget, size, color) and explain it with these facts, never to invent differences in quality, fit or material, and to show the compared items as product cards.
- Why it improves usability: Shoppers deciding between two items get a quick, accurate side-by-side answer in one message, with both products shown as clickable cards. The numbers are computed in code from the DB, so the price difference and "which has my size" are always correct.
- Files changed: `backend/tools.py` (`compare_products`, registered in `AGENT_TOOLS`), `backend/models.py` (`ComparedProduct`, `ProductComparison`), `backend/prompts/prompt.md`.
- Tested: "Compare the Basic Hoodie Big Yale and the Champion Full Zip Hood" → "The Basic is a pullover at $68.00 — $20.00 less than the Champion full-zip at $88.00. Both come in navy blue and white, while the Champion also offers charcoal gray; both have XS–XXL in stock." + 2 cards (all matches the DB). On the Crew Left Chest Hoodie page: "Which is cheaper, this or the Champion Full Zip Hood? Which one has more sizes in stock?" → "$68.00 — $20.00 cheaper… The Champion has all 6 sizes, versus 5 for this hoodie (M is out of stock)" (DB: Crew M = 0). Direct call with a fake id → listed in `not_found`. A single id → "Need at least 2".

## Agent / Backend Improvement 2
- Problem: Requests with constraints ("hoodies under $50 in XL") forced the agent to search broadly and then check inventory item by item, often running into the tool-call limit or listing items that don't actually fit. Words like "under" and "$50" were treated as search keywords, which could wrongly give zero results. And for very broad requests ("I want a Yale shirt") the agent either dumped a long list or picked for the shopper without asking.
- Change implemented:
  - **Constraint-aware search:** `search_products` gained `max_price`, `min_price` and `in_stock_size` filters, applied in SQL/Python against `catalogue.price` and `inventory.quantity > 0`. Sizes are normalized ("medium" → M, "2XL" → XXL), and an unsupported size returns a clear "not offered" note. Budget and size words (*under, below, $50, size, XL, medium…*) are no longer treated as keywords. The result note states the filters applied ("All results are price <= $50.00, in stock in size XL"). Filters-only searches work too ("anything under $35 in XXL?").
  - **Clarifying questions for broad requests:** when a search has many matches and no constraints, the tool's note flags it as broad. The prompt's new "Broad or unclear requests" section tells the agent to show 3–4 representative cards and ask **one** short narrowing question (style, color, size or budget), not to refuse or interrogate, and not to ask when the shopper already gave enough detail. It should also ask which item is meant when two products have similar names.
- Why it improves usability: Budget and size questions get complete, correct answers in one step. Every card shown fits the price and is in stock in the shopper's size, so they don't discover a sold-out size after clicking. Vague requests turn into a short guided conversation instead of an overwhelming list, which is closer to what a good store associate does.
- Files changed: `backend/tools.py` (`search_products` filters, stop words, broad-request note), `backend/prompts/prompt.md`.
- Tested: "Do you have hoodies under $50 in XL?" → exactly 1 card, Yale Sports Hoodie Tennis $45 (DB: the only hoodie ≤ $50 with XL > 0). "T-shirts under $40 in size M" → "19 T-shirts…" (DB: 19). "Navy crewneck under $60 in size L" → 17 matches, no clarifying question (DB: 17). "Any jackets under $40?" → "We don't carry any jackets under $40.00" and no cards (DB: all jackets are $98). "I want a Yale shirt" → 4 cards + one question: "Any color you're looking for?". The Problem 6 searches ("hoodies" 27, "gray crewneck" 16, "pink hoodie" 0) still give the same results.
